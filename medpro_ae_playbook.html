Section 04 — Go-To-Market Strategy

Full Cycle Sales Process

From inbound lead to closed contract. Each stage has a goal, actions, and a clear next step.

Stage 1 · Within 5 Minutes
Speed-to-Lead Response
Inbound leads go cold fast. Your goal: call within 5 minutes of form submission. Leave a voicemail + send a text + send an email. Same message, different channel. Subject line: "MedPro Quote — [Practice Name]". First email previews savings range to create urgency.
Stage 2 · Call 1 (15–20 min)
Discovery Call
Goal: understand their current setup, surface pain, qualify. Uncover: current vendor, contract status, waste types generated, pickup frequency, who makes decisions. Listen for compliance gaps, billing frustration, or service failures — these are your wedges. End with a clear next step: quote call or custom proposal.
Stage 3 · Call 2 or Same-Day
Quote Presentation
Present the custom quote built on their volume, location, and frequency. Walk them through the comparison to what they're paying now. Highlight: flat-rate pricing, no hidden fees, price lock, $33M insurance. This is where you bundle — introduce training + shredding if not already discussed. CTA: get verbal yes and send DocuSign.
Stage 4 · Objection Handling
Address & Advance
Most objections are: "I'm in a contract," "I need to think about it," "My vendor is fine," or "I need to talk to my doctor." Each has a specific response (see Objections section). Your job is to advance, not overcome. Give them a reason to act now — lock in current pricing before next rate increase.
Stage 5 · Close
Contract & Onboarding Kickoff
DocuSign sent same day as verbal yes. Follow up in 24 hrs if not signed. Once signed, schedule the compliance survey (free) and first pickup within 7–10 days. Warm handoff to service team. This moment sets the tone for the lifetime relationship — make it smooth.
Stage 6 · Post-Close (30 days)
Expand + Protect
30-day check-in call. Are they using the compliance portal? Any additional waste streams? Multi-location opportunities? Referral ask. This is where you protect your book and build pipeline. Happy customers refer other practices.

Section 06 — Objection Handling

Handle Every "No"

Click each objection to reveal the full response framework.

💬 "I'm happy with my current vendor."
Acknowledge → Reframe → Qualify:

"That's great to hear — most practices aren't actively unhappy, they just don't realize what they're overpaying until we put the numbers side by side. What I've found is that 'happy' often just means 'haven't compared lately.' Can I ask — when was the last time you audited your bill against the market? Most practices overpay by 20–30% and don't know it. Takes me five minutes to show you where you stand — worst case, you confirm you have a great deal."
💬 "I'm in a contract with my current provider."
Don't back down — get the details:

"Totally understandable — when does it expire? [Let them answer.] Got it. A few things worth knowing: first, depending on the terms, there may be flexibility to exit, especially if they've raised rates mid-contract — that's actually a breach in some agreements. Second, even if you're locked in, we can start the process now so you're ready to flip the day it expires. Can I put a proposal together so you have it ready to go? No obligation — and it might give you leverage with your current rep when they come to renew."
💬 "I need to talk to the doctor / partner first."
Qualify the decision process, then bridge:

"Of course — makes total sense. Can I ask what you'd need to feel confident recommending this to them? [Listen.] What I can do is put together a one-page summary of the savings and service comparison you can share with them — it makes the conversation really easy. And if it would help, I'm happy to jump on a five-minute call with both of you so I can answer any questions directly. What's your doctor's bandwidth like this week?"
💬 "Your price is higher than what I'm paying now."
Clarify apples-to-apples:

"Can I ask — what's included in what you're paying? [Let them answer.] The reason I ask is that a lot of quotes look cheaper on the surface because they don't include fuel surcharges, environmental fees, or stop fees that show up on the invoice later. Our quote is all-in — flat rate, no add-ons. I'd love to look at your last invoice together and compare line by line. In my experience, when we do that exercise, the gap usually disappears — and often flips."
💬 "I don't need OSHA training — my staff is already trained."
Documentation is the issue, not training:

"That's great — the question OSHA actually asks during an inspection isn't 'were your people trained?' — it's 'can you prove it?' They want dated certificates on file for every employee. If you can't produce that documentation, the training doesn't count. Our platform keeps all of that in the cloud, accessible in 30 seconds. If you've had an OSHA inspection in the last two years, how did that go on the documentation side?"
💬 "We're a small practice — we don't generate much waste."
Small generator = higher risk per incident:

"Totally understand — and we work with practices of all sizes. Actually, smaller practices are often at higher risk because they're less likely to have formal compliance infrastructure in place. And a $70K OSHA fine hits a 2-person office harder than a hospital. We have mail-back options and per-bucket pricing that work really well for low-volume generators — you only pay for what you use. Let me build you a quote based on your actual volume and I think you'll be surprised how affordable it is."
💬 "I need to think about it."
Surface the real objection:

"Of course. Can I ask — is there a specific part of this you want to think through? Sometimes 'I need to think about it' means there's a question I haven't answered well enough. Is it the pricing, the contract terms, the timing — or something else? I want to make sure you have everything you need to feel good about this."
💬 "I've never heard of MedPro Disposal."
Proof by numbers:

"Fair question — we're not as loud as Stericycle but we've been around since 2009, serve 35,000+ healthcare providers across 48 states, and carry $33M in liability insurance — more than most of the big names. We're intentionally lower-profile because we spend money on service and pricing, not marketing. If it's helpful, I can send you our customer reviews — we have more verified testimonials online than any other provider in our space. Would that help?"

Section 10 — Medical Terminology

Know the Language

Use these terms fluently in discovery and demo calls. Prospects trust AEs who speak their language.

RMW
Regulated Medical Waste. Any waste that contains infectious material or is potentially infectious. Generated by healthcare facilities and subject to strict federal and state disposal regulations.
Biohazardous Waste
Waste contaminated with blood or other potentially infectious materials (OPIM). Requires red bag containment and autoclave treatment or incineration prior to disposal.
Sharps
Any object capable of puncturing the skin — needles, syringes, scalpel blades, lancets, capillary tubes. Must be disposed of in puncture-resistant, leak-proof containers per OSHA standards.
Bloodborne Pathogens (BBP)
Microorganisms in human blood that can cause disease — HIV, HBV (Hepatitis B), HCV (Hepatitis C). OSHA's BBP Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030) requires annual employee training for all at-risk staff.
OSHA
Occupational Safety and Health Administration. Federal agency that regulates workplace safety including medical waste handling, BBP training, and PPE requirements. Fines for violations can reach $70,000+ per incident.
HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act. Federal law governing the privacy and security of protected health information (PHI). Applies to document destruction and data security practices.
PHI
Protected Health Information. Any individually identifiable health information — in paper, electronic, or oral form. Improper disposal of PHI documents is a HIPAA violation.
RCRA
Resource Conservation and Recovery Act. EPA law governing the disposal of hazardous waste including certain pharmaceutical wastes. Facilities generating hazardous pharma must comply with RCRA — non-compliance carries significant EPA fines.
DEA Scheduled Substances
Controlled substances classified by the DEA in Schedules II–V based on abuse potential. Disposal requires documented chain-of-custody. Improper disposal is a federal violation — relevant for physician offices, pharmacies, and veterinary clinics.
Cradle-to-Grave Liability
Federal principle holding generators legally responsible for their hazardous waste from point of generation through final disposal — even after transfer to a hauler. This is why MedPro taking full title on RMW is a significant liability protection for customers.
Autoclave
A steam sterilization device that renders biohazardous waste non-infectious through high-pressure steam. MedPro uses autoclave processing — an environmentally friendly alternative to incineration.
Trace Chemo Waste
Items contaminated with chemotherapy agents at low levels — gloves, tubing, bags, PPE used during chemo administration. Regulated under RCRA and requires specialized handling distinct from standard RMW.
Pathological Waste
Human or animal tissue, organs, body parts, and fluids removed during surgery or autopsy. Requires specific handling distinct from standard biohazardous waste — relevant for surgical centers, hospitals, and pathology labs.
DOT
Department of Transportation. Regulates the transport of hazardous materials including medical waste. Drivers and facilities must comply with DOT packaging, labeling, and manifesting requirements.
Manifest
A required shipping document that tracks hazardous waste from point of generation to final disposal. Part of the chain-of-custody documentation MedPro manages on behalf of customers.
OPIM
Other Potentially Infectious Materials. Body fluids beyond blood — cerebrospinal fluid, semen, vaginal secretions, pleural fluid, etc. — that require the same biohazard handling protocols as blood.
PPE
Personal Protective Equipment. Gloves, gowns, masks, face shields required when handling medical waste. Proper PPE use and disposal is part of OSHA compliance training.
Exposure Control Plan
A written OSHA-required document detailing how a facility protects employees from bloodborne pathogen exposure. Must be updated annually. MedPro's compliance platform helps facilities maintain and document this plan.
